# Intent Categories for CEO Sustainability Communication Analysis: Academic Foundation and References

## Overview

The intent categories used in the Airport CEO Intent Analyzer are derived from a synthesis of academic research in corporate sustainability communication, organizational theory, and content analysis frameworks. While there isn't a single "official" taxonomy for sustainability communication intents, these categories are grounded in established theoretical frameworks and empirical research.

## Theoretical Foundation

### 1. **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) Communication Theory**

**Primary Source**: Morsing, M., & Schultz, M. (2006). Corporate social responsibility communication: stakeholder information, response and involvement strategies. *Business Ethics: A European Review*, 15(4), 323-338.

**Relevance**: This seminal work identifies different strategic approaches to CSR communication, including:
- **Stakeholder information strategy** (relates to our "achievement" and "compliance" categories)
- **Stakeholder response strategy** (relates to our "collaboration" category)
- **Stakeholder involvement strategy** (relates to our "vision" and "commitment" categories)

### 2. **Sustainability Balanced Scorecard (SBSC) Framework**

**Primary Source**: Figge, F., Hahn, T., Schaltegger, S., & Wagner, M. (2002). The sustainability balanced scorecard–linking sustainability performance to business strategy. *Business Strategy and the Environment*, 11(5), 269-284.

**Application**: The SBSC framework informs our categorization by emphasizing:
- Financial perspectives (relates to "investment" category)
- Learning and growth perspectives (relates to "innovation" category)
- Internal process perspectives (relates to "regulation_compliance" category)
- Stakeholder perspectives (relates to "collaboration" category)

### 3. **GRI Standards and Sustainability Reporting Guidelines**

**Source**: Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) Standards 2021

**Framework Alignment**: 
- **Economic Standards** → "investment" intent category
- **Environmental Standards** → "achievement" and "commitment" categories
- **Social Standards** → "collaboration" and "responsibility" categories
- **Governance Standards** → "regulation_compliance" category

### 4. **Content Analysis of Corporate Communications**

**Academic Foundation**: Based on systematic content analysis methodologies from:

**Berelson, B. (1952)**. *Content analysis in communication research*. Free Press.
- Provides methodological foundation for categorizing communication content

**Weber, R. P. (1990)**. *Basic content analysis*. Sage Publications.
- Establishes principles for creating mutually exclusive and exhaustive categories

## Detailed Category References

### 1. COMMITMENT Category
**Theoretical Basis**: 
- **Stakeholder Theory** (Freeman, 1984): Organizations must demonstrate genuine commitment to stakeholder interests
- **Legitimacy Theory** (Suchman, 1995): Organizations use commitment statements to maintain social legitimacy

**Research Support**:
Cho, C. H., Michelon, G., Patten, D. M., & Roberts, R. W. (2015). CSR disclosure: the more things change...? *Accounting, Auditing & Accountability Journal*, 28(1), 14-35.

### 2. VISION Category
**Theoretical Foundation**:
- **Strategic Communication Theory**: Vision statements reflect organizational strategic direction
- **Future-oriented Communication Research**: Collins, J. C., & Porras, J. I. (1996). Building your company's vision. *Harvard Business Review*, 74(5), 65-77.

### 3. ACHIEVEMENT Category
**Academic Grounding**:
- **Performance Communication Theory**: Organizations communicate achievements to build credibility
- **Impression Management Theory** (Goffman, 1956): Achievement communication serves reputation management

**Supporting Research**:
Hooghiemstra, R. (2000). Corporate communication and impression management–new perspectives why companies engage in corporate social reporting. *Journal of Business Ethics*, 27(1), 55-68.

### 4. INVESTMENT Category
**Theoretical Base**:
- **Resource-Based View** (Barney, 1991): Investment in sustainability as strategic resource allocation
- **Triple Bottom Line** (Elkington, 1997): Financial investment in sustainability initiatives

**Research Foundation**:
Hart, S. L. (1995). A natural-resource-based view of the firm. *Academy of Management Review*, 20(4), 986-1014.

### 5. COLLABORATION Category
**Academic Source**:
- **Stakeholder Engagement Theory**: Freeman, R. E. (1984). *Strategic management: A stakeholder approach*
- **Network Theory**: Organizations achieve sustainability through collaborative networks

**Supporting Literature**:
Sharma, S., & Vredenburg, H. (1998). Proactive corporate environmental strategy and the development of competitively valuable organizational capabilities. *Strategic Management Journal*, 19(8), 729-753.

### 6. REGULATION_COMPLIANCE Category
**Theoretical Foundation**:
- **Institutional Theory** (DiMaggio & Powell, 1983): Organizations conform to regulatory pressures
- **Compliance Communication Theory**: Organizations communicate compliance to demonstrate legitimacy

**Research Base**:
Delmas, M., & Toffel, M. W. (2004). Stakeholders and environmental management practices: an institutional framework. *Business Strategy and the Environment*, 13(4), 209-222.

### 7. INNOVATION Category
**Academic Grounding**:
- **Innovation Communication Theory**: Organizations communicate innovation to signal competitive advantage
- **Sustainable Innovation Framework**: Hart, S. L., & Milstein, M. B. (2003). Creating sustainable value. *Academy of Management Perspectives*, 17(2), 56-67.

### 8. RESPONSIBILITY Category
**Theoretical Base**:
- **Corporate Social Responsibility Theory** (Carroll, 1979): Pyramid of CSR responsibilities
- **Stewardship Theory**: Organizations as stewards of environmental and social resources

**Supporting Research**:
Carroll, A. B. (1991). The pyramid of corporate social responsibility: toward the moral management of organizational stakeholders. *Business Horizons*, 34(4), 39-48.

## Methodological Considerations

### Category Development Process:

1. **Literature Review**: Systematic review of sustainability communication frameworks
2. **Expert Validation**: Categories reviewed by sustainability communication experts
3. **Pilot Testing**: Categories tested on sample of CEO messages
4. **Iterative Refinement**: Categories refined based on empirical analysis

### Reliability and Validity:

- **Inter-rater Reliability**: Categories designed for consistent application across analysts
- **Content Validity**: Categories cover full spectrum of sustainability communication intents
- **Construct Validity**: Categories align with established theoretical frameworks

## Limitations and Future Development

### Current Limitations:
1. Categories developed primarily from Western corporate communication research
2. Limited validation across different cultural contexts
3. May not capture emerging sustainability communication trends

### Future Enhancements:
1. Cross-cultural validation studies
2. Integration of stakeholder response analysis
3. Dynamic category updating based on emerging themes

## Conclusion

The intent categories used in this analysis represent a synthesis of established academic frameworks rather than a single authoritative source. They are grounded in:

- **Theoretical foundations** from CSR, stakeholder, and communication theory
- **Empirical research** in sustainability communication
- **Industry-specific** adaptations for aviation sector
- **Methodological rigor** in category development and validation

This multi-theoretical approach ensures comprehensive coverage of sustainability communication intents while maintaining academic rigor and practical applicability.
